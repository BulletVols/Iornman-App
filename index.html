Ironman race intensity: bike 68–75% of FTP, run at the top of Z2. Swim: targets use CSS pace from the test in week 4 (until then, go by effort). Before your first tests: use effort (RPE 1–10) and the talk test.

Where the numbers come from: FTP from the Peloton 20-minute FTP test (week 1, then each recovery week). Threshold HR from a run test: 20-minute steady efforts in weeks 4 and 8, then the 30-minute test (average HR of the last 20 minutes) from week 12.

How each coach shapes the plan

Dan Lorang (Frodeno, Haug, Charles-Barclay): consistency over hero sessions, lots of genuinely easy volume, few hard sessions, and a limit on grey-zone riding. Every 4th week drops to about 65% volume so you arrive at the next block ready to absorb it.

Olav Aleksander Bu / Kristian Blummenfelt (Norwegian method): high volume at low intensity plus a large amount of controlled work just under threshold. You don't have a lactate meter, so intervals are capped by HR, power and feel. Short rests, and you finish every rep feeling you could do another. Hard days are stacked (Thursday lift + run) so easy days stay easy.

Dr. Dan Plews (HRV, Kona age-group record): daily readiness decides whether a hard session happens. Rule: if your morning HRV is well below your 7-day normal or resting HR is 5+ bpm high, and you feel off, swap that day's key session for Z1 or rest. Long sessions double as fueling practice. His personal low-carb approach is left out on purpose: with this volume, a weight goal and a still-developing body, under-fueling is the bigger risk.

Lance Watson (Whitfield, Peter Reid): long-term development first, building threshold and skill before chasing long-course speed. That's why cycle one is about swim technique, run durability and aerobic base, with speed saved for the 2027–28 cycle.

Shins and Achilles rules

Tightness that eases once you warm up is fine. Morning-after pain above 3/10 means you skip or shorten the next run and ride instead. Sharp pain in one spot on the bone, pain that makes you limp, or pain that gets worse as you run: stop running and see a doctor (stress fractures start this way). Run volume in this plan only rises about 5 minutes a week on the long run, and weeks 1–8 use run-walk on purpose.

Fuel and weight

Aim for a slow drop of roughly 0.5–1 lb a week; that covers 20 lb well inside this cycle. Take the deficit from everyday meals, never from training fuel: eat carbs before key sessions, take carbs during any ride over 75 minutes (start around 40–60 g/hour, build toward 60–90 g/hour by spring), and eat protein soon after. If sessions start feeling flat for more than a week, eat more before you train less. A sports dietitian or your doctor can set exact targets.
