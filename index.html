Ironman race intensity: bike 103–114 W, run at the top of Z2 (158–162 bpm). Swim: targets use CSS pace from the test in week 4 (until then, go by effort). Before your first tests: use effort (RPE 1–10) and the talk test.

Where these came from: FTP 152 W from the Peloton 20-minute test in October. Threshold HR 182 bpm from the 20-minute run test on 24 Oct (max seen: 199). Threshold run pace that day was 9:13/mi. Retest at each recovery week and update these numbers.

How each coach shapes the plan

Dan Lorang (Frodeno, Haug, Charles-Barclay): consistency over hero sessions, lots of genuinely easy volume, few hard sessions, and a limit on grey-zone riding. Every 4th week drops to about 65% volume so you arrive at the next block ready to absorb it.

Olav Aleksander Bu / Kristian Blummenfelt (Norwegian method): high volume at low intensity plus a large amount of controlled work just under threshold. You don't have a lactate meter, so intervals are capped by HR, power and feel. Short rests, and you finish every rep feeling you could do another. Hard days are stacked (Thursday lift + run) so easy days stay easy.

Dr. Dan Plews (HRV, Kona age-group record): daily readiness decides whether a hard session happens. Rule: if your morning HRV is well below your 7-day normal or resting HR is 5+ bpm high, and you feel off, swap that day's key session for Z1 or rest. Long sessions double as fueling practice. His personal low-carb approach is left out on purpose: with this volume, a weight goal and a still-developing body, under-fueling is the bigger risk.

Lance Watson (Whitfield, Peter Reid): long-term development first, building threshold and skill before chasing long-course speed. That's why cycle one is about swim technique, run durability and aerobic base, with speed saved for the 2027–28 cycle.

How the year is laid out

This cycle is built around the sports calendar rather than against it. Four phases:

PhaseWeeksDatesWhat it is
Base1–18Sep 28 – Jan 31Swim season. Three coached practices a week cover the swim; Wednesday is the one hard land day.
Lacrosse season19–34Feb 1 – May 23Lacrosse is the priority. Ironman work holds at 5–6 hours. Speed block.
Summer build35–44May 24 – Aug 1The real Ironman work. Long rides outdoors, growing to 5 hours.
Peak & benchmark45–48Aug 2 – Aug 29Biggest week of the cycle, then a taper into benchmark day.

Why it is shaped this way: a peak Ironman build during lacrosse season would ask for roughly 25 hours a week on top of school and work, and it would be building toward nothing, since the race is 2028. Moving the build to summer also fixes the biggest hole in the plan — the long ride. Indoors on a Peloton it caps around 3 hours; outdoors in June with no school it reaches 5.

Swim season (weeks 3–18): three coached practices on Monday, Tuesday and Friday replace the swim sets this plan originally wrote. A coach watching your stroke beats any set written from a distance. Everything on land concentrates into one hard day, Wednesday, so the rest of the week stays genuinely easy.

Lacrosse season (weeks 19–34): lacrosse supplies the running volume and most of the intensity. The Ironman side is one long ride, one easy long run, one Peloton session — enough to hold fitness, not enough to compete with the sport. Strides and short hill sprints are the speed block: they serve lacrosse and the 7-minute-mile goal, and hills cost the shins far less than flat sprinting. Note the hours chart stays high through this phase, because lacrosse itself is counted. Total load is real even when Ironman load is light.

Summer build (weeks 35–48): school is out and the constraints disappear. Long rides move outdoors and grow toward 5 hours, long runs toward 2:30, and the weeks reach 15 hours. This block ends with a benchmark day that compares directly against the October tests, and it is the natural window for a 70.3 if you enter one.

Game or meet on a Saturday? Move the long ride to Sunday and the long run to Monday. The order matters less than getting both done.

Shins and Achilles rules

Tightness that eases once you warm up is fine. Morning-after pain above 3/10 means you skip or shorten the next run and ride instead. Sharp pain in one spot on the bone, pain that makes you limp, or pain that gets worse as you run: stop running and see a doctor (stress fractures start this way). Run volume in this plan only rises about 5 minutes a week on the long run, and weeks 1–8 use run-walk on purpose.

Fuel and weight

Aim for a slow drop of roughly 0.5–1 lb a week; that covers 20 lb well inside this cycle. Take the deficit from everyday meals, never from training fuel: eat carbs before key sessions, take carbs during any ride over 75 minutes (start around 40–60 g/hour, build toward 60–90 g/hour by spring), and eat protein soon after. If sessions start feeling flat for more than a week, eat more before you train less. A sports dietitian or your doctor can set exact targets.
